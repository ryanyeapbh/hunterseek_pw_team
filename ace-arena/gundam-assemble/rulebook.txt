Overview

Welcome to Gundam Assemble!

This fast
-
paced, tactical miniatures game puts players directly into the cockpits of legendary
Mobile Suits from across the Gundam franchise. Build squads of your favorite units before
diving into lightning
-
fast skirmishes
—
or relive iconic battles through
cinematic story scenarios
drawn from Gundam’s most memorable conflicts. Combine Mobile Suits from across every
Gundam era to create unforgettable battles no timeline has seen before.

Each game of Gundam Assemble revolves around a unique scenario. Some are open
-
ended
tactical skirmishes; others recreate pivotal moments from Gundam’s vast history. Scenarios
define setup and victory conditions
—
players earn victory points for defeating ene
mies, capturing
key Objectives, and completing mission
-
specific goals. When the smoke clears, the player with
the highest score claims victory!

f.

Other unit or scenario

Gameplay

Object of the Game

Games of Gundam Assemble consist of two Phases, each including 10 rounds, for a total of 20
rounds per game. Players will activate their units during each round to capture or defend
Objectives, complete scenario
-
specific goals, and
—
of course
—
defeat enemy M
obile Suits.

Players will earn victory points throughout the course of the game by defeating enemy units and
Garrisons, rescuing allied Garrisons, and by completing scenario goals. The player with the
most victory points at the end of a game is considered the winner.

The Timeline (TL) System

Games of Gundam Assemble revolve around the Timeline system. This system represents the
passage of time during battle and determines when players are able to activate their units.
Understanding how units interact with the Timeline is key to managing gamepl
ay. Your time
during each game is limited, and careful management of your Timeline will dramatically improve
your odds of victory.

The Timeline Track

The Timeline track consists of 10 spaces representing the 10 rounds of a Phase. Players begin
the game by placing a Timeline token on this track for each of their units. Units will move up the
Timeline as they’re activated.

The Timeline track is divided into two sides: one for each player. Be sure to add Timeline tokens
to your side of the track.

Setting up the Timeline track

Players will place a Timeline token on the track for each of their units at the start of the game,
based on each unit’s starting Timeline value. If more than one of your units is placed on the
same space of the Timeline, stack the tokens on top of each oth
er in your preferred order. Units
on top of the stack will act before units underneath.

(or when there are no more tokens present on any rounds in the current Phase), the Phase
ends.

appropriate number of victory points.

Begin the second Phase.

●

If this is the second Phase of the game:

○

Resolve any scenario Objectives that correspond to the second Phase and score
the appropriate number of victory points.

○

Compare victory point totals and determine the winner.

or adjacent to the

Activating your Units

When you begin a round containing one or more of your units, you’ll have an opportunity to
activate those units. When activating a unit, you must take one Primary Action and may take
other actions as defined below.

Advancing is a unit’s basic move action. When a unit Advances, it may move up to three hexes.
Terrain and enemies will impact movement. See “Movement Modifiers
.
” Some cards and
abilities will also allow a unit to Advance or to take other movement actions. These effects are in
addition to a unit’s normal Advance action.

Dash

Spend two TL to move up to two hexes. Terrain and enemies will impact movement.

Energize

Spend two TL to gain an Energy by placing an Energy token on the appropriate unit card. Units
can have multiple Energy at a time.

Rescue

Spend two TL to Rescue an adjacent allied Garrison and score two victory points.

Additional Actions

Many abilities allow units to take additional actions, allowing them to take multiple Primary
Actions in the same activation. Typically, units can take one additional action of the type
specified by the effect for each use of an ability that allows additio
nal actions.

Actions that have their Timeline cost reduced to 0 by an ability are also considered additional
actions of their type. A unit that Dashes at Timeline 0, for example, is considered to be making
an additional Dash and has not used up their Primary Action for

that activation.

Use Command Abilities

You may use each Command ability once.

Play a Tactics Card

Players may play up to one Tactics card per activation. Each type of Tactics card has specific
rules dictating when it can be played. See “Cards and Abilities
.
”

Moving

To move a unit, move it one hex in any direction from its current position. Continue to move
forward one hex at a time until you’ve moved your maximum distance or you wish to end your
movement. You may end your movement at any point during the move; you do

not need to
move your full allowable distance. If you choose to move, you may not end your movement in
the same hex where you started.

You may not move through hexes containing enemy units, enemy Garrisons, enemy Carriers, or
enemy structures (such as Bases) but you may move through hexes containing allies or allied
structures. You may not end your movement in the same hex as another enem
y, ally, or
structure unless otherwise specified.

You may not take other actions during your movement. For example: you cannot move one hex,
make an attack, and then resume moving. You must complete your movement before taking
another action.

For units that occupy multiple hexes, all hexes belonging to the unit must be at the same
elevation when ending any move action.

Critical Hits

Any time a player rolls a 9 or 10 on one or more attack dice, they score a critical hit. Critical hits
succeed against a target regardless of any Accuracy modifiers.

Additionally, all weapons have a critical hit effect that triggers when a player rolls a critical hit
with that weapon. (Some critical hit effects require rolling multiple critical hits to activate.)

Critical
hit effects may raise a weapon’s damage, inflict a status effect, destroy an enemy Upgrade, or
any number of other effects.

Critical hit effects on weapons can only be activated once per attack, regardless of how many
critical hits are rolled, unless otherwise specified by the effect.

Accuracy modifiers do not affect critical hit values; a player must still roll a 9 or 10 on one or
more attack dice in order to score a critical hit, unless otherwise specified.

Damage and Shields

After you’ve determined the number of hits scored by an attack, you’ll need to determine the
amount of damage dealt. Each hit against an enemy target deals one damage, but some units
may reduce incoming damage through the use of Shields. Each active Shield

prevents one
point of damage from an attack. Any remaining damage reduces the target unit’s hit points.

Shields are applied against incoming damage after all other effects resolve. Cards and abilities
that also reduce damage will do so before the damage impacts any Shields.

Critical hit effects apply even if Shields prevent all damage from an attack.

Some critical hit effects and other abilities will increase the amount of damage dealt by an
attack. Effects and abilities that say an attack deals “Damage

Combat Damage:
All damage dealt outside of an attack or by an effect that is not the
result of resolving hits from an attack, critical hits that modify attack damage, or Fracture is
considered non
-
combat damage. This includes collision damage.

Ongoing:

Ongoing abilities are active at the start of the game and remain so until the game is
completed.

Phase:
Each game of Gundam Assemble includes two Phases, each of which consists of 10
rounds. Players will score victory points at the end of each Phase as dictated by the scenario.

Pull:
A unit affected by Pull is moved toward the source of the Pull up to the number of hexes
specified by the effect. This movement is not affected by typical movement modifiers. An enemy
that is already adjacent to you at the same elevation cannot be Pulled.
Pull can cause collision
damage. See “Pull
.
”

Push:

A unit affected by Push is moved away from the source of the Push up to the number of
hexes specified by the effect. This movement is not affected by typical movement modifiers.
Push can cause collision damage. See “Push
.
”

Range:
All weapons have a range value. A weapon cannot be used to make an attack beyond
its stated range. Many abilities also have a stated range that players must take into account.
Attacks and abilities with a stated range also require a player to have Line of
Sight to their
target. See “Range
.
”

Reinforcements:
Some units are able to deploy Reinforcements to the battlefield. When this
occurs, tokens are placed both on the board and on the Timeline track to represent the new
unit. Reinforcements are considered units. When a Reinforcement is defeated, remove the
to
ken from the game board and remove the corresponding Timeline Token from the Timeline.
Reinforcements cannot be redeployed.

Remote Weapon System (RWS):
Some units come equipped with abilities that allow them to
deploy Remote Weapon Systems to the battlefield. These abilities and the abilities of the
systems themselves are described on the relevant unit and Remote Weapon System cards.
Remote Weapon Systems

obey the following rules:

●

RWS may only deploy or reposition to an empty hex.

●

RWS do not block Line of Sight or movement.

●

Units may not end their movement in the same hex as an RWS.

●

RWS do not count as units, do not cause Engagement, and do not count when
contesting Objectives.

●

RWS can be attacked and destroyed.

●

RWS can be Pushed or Pulled and cause collision damage.

●

RWS cannot be affected by status effects.

●

When an RWS is destroyed, it returns to the supply of the unit that deployed it and may
be deployed again during a future activation.

●

Units cannot deploy more RWS than they have available in their supply.

Repair:
When a unit Repairs, it gains a number of Hit Points up to the amount specified by the
ability. Units cannot gain more than their maximum number of Hit Points.

Reposition:

Some abilities allow a player to reposition units, Garrisons, or Remote Weapon
Systems. When a player repositions something, remove the object from its current hex on the
board and place it on a new hex as directed by the ability causing the repositioning
.
Repositioning is considered a movement action.

Reserve:
Players begin with their units off the board, in their Reserve, and units return to a
player’s Reserve when defeated. Units in a player’s Reserve cannot be targeted by attacks or
gain status effects, and some abilities printed on those units may not be act
ive until they are
deployed.

Response:
Response abilities can only be used in response to a specific situation or trigger
specified by the ability. When a player uses a Response ability, resolve the ability in its entirety
before resuming other actions.

Round:
Resolving a single space on the Timeline track is considered a round. There are 10
rounds in each Phase of gameplay. The number of players that act during a round is dependent
on the position of units on the Timeline.

Scenario Goals:
All Gundam Assemble scenarios will have some number of scenario goals
that players can complete during gameplay. Refer to your chosen scenario for details on its
scenario goals. See “Scenarios, Goals, and Victory Points
.
”

Setup:
Setup abilities affect the game during the setup phase. Resolve these abilities in order
beginning with the first player to act during the game.

Shield:
Each Shield a unit possesses prevents one damage from an attack or ability. Shields
become deactivated when they prevent damage and will not prevent further damage until
reactivated. Deactivated Shields are reactivated the next time the unit possessing the
m is
activated.

Signature Tactics Cards:

Each unit comes with a Signature Tactics card that can only be used
by that unit. The unit named on the card is considered the source of any effects produced by
these cards.

Speed:
Upgrades and effects that increase Speed increase how far a unit can move when
Advancing. A unit can move one additional hex for each point of Speed.

Squad:
A player’s squad includes all units and Carriers they intend to use during gameplay.

Slow:
The next time a Slowed unit Advances, it moves 0 hexes instead. See “Status Effects
.
”

Status Effects:
Many cards and abilities apply status effects to their target, represented by
corresponding status effect tokens. Status effects may only affect units, they do not affect
Garrisons or Remote Weapon Systems. See “Status Effects
.
”

Strength:
Each weapon has a Strength value indicating the number of attack dice to roll when
making an attack with that weapon. See “Attacking
.
”

Structure:

Many scenarios will include Bases or other structures belonging to one or both
players. Rules for structures other than Bases will be provided with each specific scenario. In
general, enemy structures block both movement and Line of Sight unless otherwise

specified.

Swap:

Some abilities allow units to swap with each other. When this occurs, exchange the
position of each miniature involved in the swap. Swapping is considered a movement action.

Terrain Movement Penalties:

The term ‘terrain movement penalties’ refers both to the
movement penalty imposed by going up in elevation and to movement penalties caused by
special terrain types like water.

Timeline Penalty:
When a unit is defeated, it immediately moves two spaces up on the
Timeline track as a penalty, representing the time needed to repair the unit before it returns to
battle.

Trait:
All units have one or more traits indicating their pilot, faction affiliation, or other unique
characteristics. These traits are referred to by abilities and used to determine which Tactics
cards a player can include in their deck.
