### **Overview & Winning the Game**
Hi, I'm Justin Garary, the lead designer for **Gundam Assemble**, and today we're going to learn how to play using the starter set to go through all of the core rules. Now this video is going to be divided into sections so you can easily jump around and find the rules you're looking for, but let's start with **how to win**. You win Gundam Assemble by getting the **most victory points** at the end of two phases. You earn victory points by **defeating enemy mobile suits**, **defeating enemy garrisons**, **rescuing allied garrisons**, and **completing scenario objectives**.

---

### **Setup & Terrain**
The first step in playing Gundam Assemble is **choosing your scenario**. For today's game, we'll be using the starter set scenario, **Sleeping Leviathan**. Scenarios tell you where to place the terrain, where to place objectives and special tokens, and give you any special rules that you need to know in order to play. First, **place the map** determined by the scenario. Next, **place the terrain**. Use the included **3D hex terrain** to visually represent elevation: light-colored pieces are at height called **level one**, and dark-colored pieces are called **level two** (we'll go into why elevation is important later).

---

### **Tokens, Squad Assembly & Tactics Deck**
**Tokens** is a general term for markers that indicate various in-game elements. Start with the **base tokens** for both the blue and red players. In a standard game of Gundam Assemble, units start at their base when deploying for the first time and when returning to the game after being defeated. Next, place the **garrison tokens** for both blue and red. Then place the **neutral objective tokens**. After that, place the **upgrade tokens**. Upgrade tokens should be randomized and placed face down so neither you nor your opponent know what's on the bottom. Finally, you're ready to begin.

Now it's time to **assemble your squad**. A typical game of Gundam Assemble uses a **three-unit squad**, but some games use five units or more. The rules for assembling your squad are simple: you can use **only one copy of the same unit card**, though you can have multiple copies of the same Gundam figure. So if you want to have a squad of entirely **RX-78-2s**, you can do that so long as their unit cards are different. The only exception to this rule is **mass-produced units**. Mass-produced units have a mass-produced tag on them, and you can have as many of those as you want, like the **Ball** unit that you see here.

Once you've assembled your squad, it's time to build your **tactics deck**. Tactics decks include some of the most powerful effects in the game that are all **single use**. To build a tactics deck, you must choose **exactly nine cards**; each card may include only one copy. Some tactics cards require you to have specific units or specific factions in your squad—for example, **War Edge** requires you to have **Gundam Epyon** in your squad. Once you've built your tactics deck and your squad, you're ready to play.

---

### **The Timeline**
The heart of Gundam Assemble is the **timeline**. At the beginning of the game, place your unit tokens on the timeline based on the **starting TL number** on your unit card. If two of your units have the same timeline number, place them on top of each other in any order; the unit on top of the stack will go first when that timeline is reached. At the beginning of the game, the unit with the **lowest timeline number** will go first. If both players have a unit on the same timeline at the very first turn of the game, **roll a die** to determine which unit goes first. After that, players will alternate units on the same timeline based on which player played last.

---

### **Unit Cards, Tactics Cards & Movement**
Now let's talk about **unit cards** and **tactics cards**. A unit card defines all of the abilities of your mobile suit: it includes your **starting HP**, the **victory points** that unit is worth when defeated, and your **starting timeline**. In addition, it includes all of the powers that you can use during the game, including your **attack abilities**, any **ongoing powers**, and your **energy power**. 

Tactics cards come in **three varieties**:
- **Command cards** can be used during your turn in addition to other powers.
- An **Attack card** replaces your attack for the turn.
- **Response cards** can be used anytime their condition is met, including during an opponent's turn.

Let me talk about **advancing**. When it's your unit's turn to act, you may advance and take a primary action in either order. On the very first turn, you'll begin the game on your base. When taking an advance action, you may move **up to three hexes**. If you're moving to a **higher level of terrain**, that counts as an additional move. When moving from high terrain to lower terrain, it works just like normal.

---

### **Primary Actions**
Other than attacking, all primary actions **cost two timeline**. To take a primary action, first advance your unit's token along the timeline by the appropriate amount.
1. **Dashing:** Allows you to move up to **two additional spaces** following the same rules as advancing.
2. **Rescue an Allied Garrison:** Allows you to remove an adjacent allied garrison from the board to score **two victory points**.
3. **Energize:** Place an **energy token** onto your unit card. Once you've energized a unit, you can use its **energy power** by simply removing the energy token from the unit card and doing whatever the ability says.

---

### **Attacking, Range, Line of Sight & Elevation**
Now let's get into my favorite part of Gundam Assemble: **attacking**. When you want to make an attack, the first thing to do is **check the range number** on your attack ability and see if you have **line of sight** to your target. In this case, Char's Zaku Bazooka has a range of four, which is sufficient to hit RX-78; however, because there is terrain in between those units, he does not have clear line of sight. **Higher level terrain** and **enemy units** both block line of sight. Once you've checked line of sight, you pay the timeline cost for your attack, and you're ready to roll.

After checking range, line of sight, and paying your timeline cost, it's time to roll. In this case, **Gundam Epyon** will be using his beam sword against **Barbatos**. When making an attack, **fours or better** are considered hits. 
- **High Ground:** When you hit from high ground, you get **+1 to the number on your die roll**, which means Epyon only needs **threes or better** to hit Barbatos.
- **Low Ground:** If you are attacking from lower ground to higher ground, you get a **-1 accuracy modifier** to your roll, which means you need **fives or better**.

Let's see what happens: not only do all of these dice hit, but also I've rolled a **critical attack**. Any **nines or tens** rolled in an attack make it a critical attack, which means you may activate that particular attack's critical ability. The beam sword does damage plus one, so in addition to hitting all seven dice, this will do one more damage for **eight total damage** placed onto Barbatos.

### **Defeating Units & Status Effects**
When you deal damage to a unit equal to or greater than its **HP value**, that unit is **defeated**. When a unit is defeated:
- Remove it from the board.
- Clear all damage and status effects off of its unit card.
- **Advance its token two spaces along the timeline** to account for the time it takes to repair.
- When that unit returns to the board, it will **respawn at the base** just like at the beginning of the game.
- Your opponent gains **victory points** equal to the VP value on that unit's unit card.

**Enemy garrisons** can be defeated just like enemy units. To defeat an enemy garrison, you only need to do **one point of damage**. Remove it from the board, and enemy garrisons are worth **two victory points**.

**Status effects** are temporary negative abilities that can be applied to your mobile suit during the game:
- **Slow:** Forces your unit to escape their next advance.
- **Disarm:** Forces you to roll all hits on your next attack and makes crits impossible.
- **Fracture:** The next time you take three or more damage, you take an additional three damage.

Each time a status effect is triggered, or if your unit is defeated, they are removed from your unit card.

---

### **Push/Pull Abilities & Upgrades**
You can use **push and pull abilities** during a game of Gundam Assemble to manipulate the battlefield. When pushing an enemy unit, you must move it further away with each movement. When moving into an empty space, they end their move there. If you would push them into a piece of higher terrain, that unit ends its move immediately and takes **two damage**. If you push an enemy unit into another unit, **both units take two damage** (the same applies to pulling, just in reverse).

**Upgrades** are an incredible way to power up your mobile suit for the rest of the game. To pick up an upgrade, simply end your move on top of one of the upgrade tokens, reveal its special power, and add it to your unit card. The three types of upgrades in Gundam Assemble are:
- **Strength:** Allows you to roll an **additional die** anytime you attack.
- **Speed:** Allows you to move an **additional space** every time you advance.
- **Shield:** Absorbs the **first point of damage** you take, at which point the shield becomes deactivated and may reactivate the next time you take a turn.

Some units have **ongoing abilities**; those abilities are available during the entire game, so check your unit card to determine which abilities your unit might have.

---

### **Engagement & Objectives**
Whenever two enemy units are adjacent to each other, they are considered to be **engaged**. Enemy units that are engaged **must attack each other** if they attack at all, and if you start a movement adjacent to an enemy, your movement is at **-1** (units on different elevations are not considered engaged).

To **contest an objective**, your unit must end on or adjacent to the objective location.
- If there are no enemy units present, you place your **control marker** on top of the neutral objective.
- If you successfully contest an objective already controlled by your opponent, simply remove their token and return the objective to neutral.
- When contesting an objective with enemy units on or adjacent to the objective, you must have a **majority of units** next to the objective in order to successfully contest it.

---

### **End of Phase & Winning**
Sometimes when paying an activation cost, the cost will move you past round 10 of the timeline. In this case, just keep moving your timeline token, starting at one. So for example, to play a timeline cost of three when you're starting at timeline 9, you'll move 1, 2, 3 spaces and end on timeline 2. 

Once all units have moved past round 10, you reach the **end of the phase**. During the end of the phase, you score points equal to the objectives you control according to the scenario rules, then draw **three additional tactics cards** and you're ready to begin phase two. Once you've reached the end of phase two, you score objectives one more time, then the player with the **most victory points wins the game**—and now you know how to play Gundam Assemble!
